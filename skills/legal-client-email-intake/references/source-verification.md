# Source verification

## What counts as a legal or procedural proposition

Anything asserting what the law, a procedure or an authority requires, permits,
blocks or typically does. Examples of propositions that need a source:

- "Closing an asylum application obliges the Authority to issue a re-entry visa."
- "The Authority tends to refuse re-entry visas while an asylum claim is pending."
- "Documents must be apostilled and notarially translated."
- "The B/1 track runs 63 months before an A5 application."
- Any number of days, months or years attached to a right or a deadline.

The following do **not** need a source: restating a fact the client supplied,
describing what the firm itself did or will do, procedural courtesy, and
scheduling.

## Source hierarchy

1. The Population and Immigration Authority procedure itself, cited by
   procedure number, title and version date, with applicability checked against
   the client's actual permit type.
2. Primary legislation and regulations.
3. Binding and current case law.
4. Official gov.il guidance pages.

Secondary commentary, prior emails from the firm, and recollection are not
sources. A procedure that has been superseded is not a source.

## Holding reply rule

If a proposition material to the answer cannot be verified against a current
official source, do not guess and do not soften the guess with hedging language.
Instead:

- Record the proposition internally under unverified propositions.
- Set confidence to low.
- Produce a neutral holding reply.

A holding reply confirms receipt, restates the client's question accurately so
they can see it was understood, states what the firm is doing next and by when,
asks for any facts genuinely needed, and commits to a substantive answer after
verification. It contains no legal conclusion.

This is the correct output, not a failure. A holding reply sent today is
recoverable; a wrong procedural assertion acted upon is often not.

## Interaction with the client's own prior correspondence

Where the firm has already stated a position in the thread, that position may be
restated as "as we wrote", but it does not become verified merely because the
firm said it before. If the client is now challenging that position, as opposed
to asking for clarification, treat it as requiring verification.
