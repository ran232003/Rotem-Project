# Safety and escalation

Run this before drafting. If any trigger fires, the draft is not a normal reply.

> **Awaiting the firm's decision.** Rotem's own version of this procedure sets
> the deadline trigger at **14 days**, not 72 hours, and covers hearings,
> interviews, appointments, expiring status and travel. Hers also separates the
> two things this section merges: her trigger means "never auto-send", a
> statement about who approves, whereas the heading below also stops the agent
> writing a substantive draft at all. Widening 72 hours to 14 days without
> splitting those apart would turn most immigration replies into content-free
> acknowledgements, so the number is left as it is until she decides. Until then
> the shorter window is in force and the agent is more permissive than the
> firm's written policy.

## Escalate to the principal lawyer, do not draft substantively

- Detention, an existing removal order, or refusal of entry in progress.
- Any deadline falling within 72 hours, including a hearing or a flight already booked.
- A threat, an accusation of malpractice, or a complaint about the firm.
- Contact from an adverse party, or from a party whose interests conflict with an existing client.
- Media or regulator enquiries.
- Suspected fraud, forged documents, or a request to misstate facts to the Authority.
- Anything involving a minor's status where a parent's authorisation is unclear.

For these, produce a short acknowledgement only, confirming receipt and stating
that the matter is being reviewed with priority, and set the internal approval
level to principal lawyer review.

## Identity gate

Do not include case information, document contents, or status details until the
sender's identity and authorisation are adequate.

A sender writing on behalf of a client, such as an agency or a family member, is
authorised only for matters where that authorisation already appears in the
thread or the client record. When in doubt, reply on process only and ask for
written confirmation from the client.

Note that the sender is frequently not the client. An agency may refer several
unrelated clients from a single address, so identity must be resolved per matter
and never inferred from the email domain alone.

## Sensitive data

Treat identity, health, family, immigration, criminal, financial and minors'
information as sensitive. Request the minimum necessary. Do not ask for full
passports, biometrics or financial records over ordinary email where a secure
channel exists. Never reproduce passwords, card data or access codes.
