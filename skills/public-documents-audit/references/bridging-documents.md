# Bridging documents

The document that closes a gap, by the kind of gap. Named here so the audit
proposes a specific instrument rather than "further documentation".

| Gap | Bridging document |
| --- | --- |
| Surname differs between birth certificate and passport | Marriage certificate, divorce certificate, or a name-change certificate |
| Given name or its spelling differs | Name-change certificate, or an official confirmation of identity from the country of origin |
| Transliteration differs between countries | An official confirmation from the issuing authority, or a notarised affidavit of identity |
| Status between a divorce and a later marriage | Personal-status certificate, CENOMAR or equivalent, confirmation of no intervening marriage, foreign population-registry confirmation, or a consular certificate |
| Widowhood not evidenced | Death certificate of the spouse, plus a personal-status certificate for the period after it |
| Period of residence in a third country | Residence or work permit from that country, and a police clearance certificate covering the stay |
| A certificate exists but predates the file by many years | A recent extract or re-issue from the same register |
| Apostille attached to a covering letter rather than the certificate | A fresh apostille on the certificate itself |
| No apostille possible because the state is not a party to the Hague Convention | Consular authentication by the Israeli mission in that state |

Where none of these fits, say what the gap is and that the instrument is
undetermined. Naming the wrong document sends the client abroad for nothing.

## Israeli requirements that recur

A certificate in a foreign language generally needs a notarised Hebrew
translation. Whether a given document needs one is the firm's call, and the
procedure does not record the rule, so treat it as the firm's practice and put
the authority in `open_questions`.
